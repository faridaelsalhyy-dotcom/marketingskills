# Brand World and Research

TYPE B sells a world, a language and a belonging, not just garments. The recognizable thing is the **personality**.

## Brand World Canvas (fill once, keep alive in `04_BRAND`)

| Element | Question | TYPE B answer (founder to confirm) |
|---------|----------|------------------------------------|
| Girl | Who is she, precisely? | Organized chaos, impulsive but self-aware, rebellious without trying, sarcastic, feminine |
| Tension | What contradiction defines her? | Planner vs. last-minute; polish vs. mess |
| Language | Words, phrases, jokes she uses | Build a glossary; ban list for off-brand phrases |
| Visual codes | Palette, type, symbols, texture, framing | Define 5–7 repeatable codes |
| Behaviors | What does she do? | Late for everything, outfit-spirals, group-chat energy |
| Rituals | Repeating community moments | Drop day, "outfit of the chaos" |
| Product expression | How each piece carries the world | Naming, tags, packaging notes, details |
| Touchpoints | Site, packaging, unboxing, DMs, WhatsApp, delivery | One voice across all |
| Belonging | Why does she feel seen, not sold to? | Content that names her moments |
| Boundaries | What TYPE B never does | Write them down |

Test: cover the logo. Would someone still know it's TYPE B?

## Source Study Method

For each reference the founder listed (Brand Blueprint, Brand House, Layer by Layer, Let's Talk About Brands, Emily Loves, Brand House "How to Build a Brand World"):

`Source → Key principles → Framework → Practical application → TYPE B adaptation → Test to run`

Do not summarize for its own sake. Verify any quoted claim against the actual article, cite with link and date, and keep your own conclusions separate from the author's.

Distinctions to hold (from the founder's reading list):
- **Brand strategy ≠ design strategy ≠ marketing strategy:** different owners, timelines and expertise.
- **World-building vs belonging:** worlds attract attention; belonging creates community and repeat behavior.
- **Market change:** DTC economics today differ from earlier years (higher acquisition costs, platform dependence), so plan for margin and retention, not just launch spikes.

## Research Workflow

| Track | Questions | Output (in `11_RESEARCH`) |
|-------|-----------|----------------------------|
| Gen-Z behavior | How do they discover, trust, buy, return? | Insight notes with quotes and sources |
| Egypt market | Purchasing power, local competitors and pricing, fabric/production reality, COD and payments, ad costs, trends, seasonality | Egypt fact base with source + date per claim; "assumption" tag when unsourced |
| Competitors | Positioning, price ladder, content mix, ad library, drop cadence | Competitor grid refreshed monthly (use Meta Ad Library for active ads) |
| Fashion trends | Silhouettes, fabrics, color, cultural moments | Trend log tied to drop planning |
| Case studies | Brands that built belonging, failures | One-page lessons with TYPE B application |

Source priority: official platform docs → government/statistics → academic → established research/consulting → reputable fashion/business press → primary interviews → expert analysis. Label old benchmarks with their date.

Also use `customer-research`, `competitors`, `competitor-profiling` and `social` skills for execution.

## Website / UX Principles

Information architecture: Home · Shop · Drop · Product · Brand World · About · Journal/Content · Community · FAQ · Shipping · Returns.

Product page flow: **Why this exists → what it feels like → why you want it → details → fit/size → styling → social proof → buy.** Include COD/shipping/returns clarity early. The site should communicate product + personality + world, not product + buy button.

## Visual System for Dashboards

Rebellious, feminine, slightly chaotic, editorial, sarcastic, playful, intentionally imperfect, fashion-forward. Professional without sterile SaaS blandness: bold type, a restrained accent palette, playful labels (e.g. "FIX / TEST / WAIT"), legible tables first.
