# Color Science for Styling

Deep reference for color analysis, palette building, and color harmony.

## Color Models Stylists Should Know

| Model | Used for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **RYB wheel** | Traditional art/fashion color theory | Basis of most styling harmony rules |
| **Munsell (Hue, Value, Chroma)** | The three dimensions of color | Maps directly to temperature, depth, clarity in personal color analysis |
| **Pantone (TCX / TPG)** | Communicating textile colors to factories | TCX = cotton textile standards; always specify physical standard for lab dips |
| **RGB / HEX** | Screens, e-commerce, digital lookbooks | Screen colors vary by device; never approve production color from a screen |
| **CMYK** | Print (lookbooks, hangtags) | Converted colors shift; proof physically |
| **CIELAB (L\*a\*b\*)** | Measuring color difference in production | ΔE (delta E) under ~1 is generally imperceptible; brands typically set tolerances around 1.0–2.0 for lab dip approval |

## Personal Color Analysis in Depth

### Step 1: Temperature (hue)

- **Warm** — golden, peachy, or olive-golden undertone; hair often has gold, copper, or red highlights; eyes often warm brown, hazel, amber, or green with gold flecks
- **Cool** — pink, rosy, or blue-red undertone; hair often ash, blue-black, or platinum; eyes often cool blue, grey, or dark brown with no golden cast
- **Neutral** — mixed; can usually wear moderate versions of both, avoiding extremes

Undertone is independent of skin depth — every depth, from very fair to very deep, can be warm, cool, or neutral.

### Step 2: Value (depth)

Compare overall lightness of hair, skin, and eyes combined:

- **Light** — flattered by light-to-medium values; very dark colors near the face may overpower
- **Deep** — flattered by rich, dark values; very pale colors may wash out
- **Medium** — medium values anchor the palette

### Step 3: Chroma (clarity)

- **Clear / bright** — high-contrast features, eyes with sparkle; flattered by saturated, clean colors
- **Soft / muted** — features blend together; flattered by greyed, dusty, blended colors; bright neons may overpower

### Step 4: Contrast

Photograph the person in greyscale. The difference between darkest (often hair or eyes) and lightest (often skin or eye whites) shows natural contrast. Match outfit contrast to it.

### Drape Test Protocol

1. Natural daylight (north-facing window ideal), no makeup, hair covered with a neutral grey or white cloth
2. Neutral grey or white top
3. Compare pairs of fabric drapes under the chin, one dimension at a time:
   - Temperature: warm vs cool version of the same color (coral vs fuchsia, gold vs silver lamé)
   - Value: light vs deep version
   - Chroma: bright vs muted version
4. Look for: evenness of skin tone, shadows under eyes, redness, clarity of eyes, jawline definition
5. Record the winning side of each pair; the combination indicates the sub-season

## Color Harmony Math

On a 12-hue color wheel (hues 30° apart):

- **Complementary** — 180° apart
- **Split complementary** — base + 150° + 210°
- **Analogous** — 3 adjacent hues within 60°
- **Triadic** — 120° apart
- **Tetradic / square** — 90° apart (hard to wear; use one as dominant)

### Proportion of Color

- **60-30-10** — dominant / secondary / accent
- **80/20** for complementary schemes — one color dominates, the other accents
- Brighter and warmer colors appear larger and heavier than cool, muted colors; use less of them to balance

## Optical Effects

- **Simultaneous contrast** — a color looks different depending on neighbors (grey looks warmer next to blue, cooler next to orange)
- **Advancing vs receding** — warm, light, bright, and shiny colors advance; cool, dark, and matte colors recede
- **Metamerism** — two fabrics match under one light source but not another; approve colors under the retailer's standard light box (D65 daylight, TL84 store light, A incandescent)
- **Fabric changes color** — the same dye looks different on silk, cotton, wool, and polyester because of sheen and texture

## Neutral Families

Neutrals carry temperature too:

- **Warm neutrals** — camel, cream, ivory, chocolate, olive, warm grey, tan
- **Cool neutrals** — pure white, black, charcoal, navy, cool grey, taupe
- **Universal-ish neutrals** — soft white, mid-grey, denim blue, soft navy — flatter most people
