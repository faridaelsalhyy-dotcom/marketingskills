# Proportion & Silhouette Reference

Detailed guidance for balancing silhouettes, directing the eye, and building outfit formulas.

## Measuring the Body for Styling

Take these proportions (in addition to fit measurements):

| Measure | How | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Shoulder vs hip width** | Compare shoulder width to widest hip point | Upper vs lower body balance |
| **Waist definition** | Waist ÷ hip, waist ÷ bust | Whether waist-defining styles follow the body |
| **Torso vs leg length** | Crown-to-hip vs hip-to-floor; a balanced figure is often ~1:1 at the hip, with legs ~half of height | Whether to lengthen torso or legs |
| **Neck length** | Relative to face and shoulders | Neckline and collar choices |
| **Bone structure / scale** | Wrist circumference, facial features size | Print, accessory, and detail scale |
| **Height** | Petite (often <160 cm / 5'3"), average, tall (often >175 cm / 5'9") | Garment length, scale, layering |

Record the client's goals for each area: highlight, balance, or no preference.

## Lines

| Line type | Effect | Garment examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertical** | Lengthens, slims | Button plackets, princess seams, long cardigans, pinstripes, V-necks, long necklaces |
| **Horizontal** | Widens, shortens, stops the eye | Boat necks, belts, hemlines, horizontal stripes, color blocks |
| **Diagonal** | Dynamic, slimming if long | Wrap dresses, asymmetric hems, lapels |
| **Curved** | Soft, feminine, adds volume | Scalloped edges, round necks, peplums, rounded hems |

## Silhouette Families

| Silhouette | Shape | Works for |
|---|---|---|
| **A-line** | Fitted top, flares down | Balancing hips; universally easy |
| **H / column** | Straight up and down | Elongating; rectangle shapes; minimal styles |
| **X / hourglass** | Defined waist, balanced shoulder and hem | Emphasizing waist |
| **V / inverted triangle** | Wide shoulder, narrow hem | Adding shoulder presence; 80s tailoring |
| **O / cocoon** | Volume in the middle, narrow at ends | Fashion-forward, relaxed; balance with slim legs |
| **Trapeze** | Narrow shoulder, very wide hem | Youthful, 60s, playful |

## Balancing Volume

**One area of volume at a time**, unless creating an intentional oversized look.

| Volume on top | Pair with |
|---|---|
| Oversized sweater, puff sleeves, boxy jacket | Slim trousers, pencil skirt, leggings, straight jeans |
| Volume on bottom | Pair with |
| Wide-leg trousers, full midi skirt, balloon pants | Fitted or tucked top, cropped jacket, bodysuit |

Oversized-on-oversized works when: fabric drapes (not stiff), one piece shows a body line (wrist, ankle, neck), and the waist is suggested.

## Hemlines and Lengths

- A hem stops the eye — place it at a narrow point (above/below the knee, mid-calf narrowing, ankle) rather than the widest point of the calf or thigh
- **Cropped trousers** end at the narrowest part of the ankle
- **Midi skirts** read best with shoes that continue the line (pointed toe, heel, or matching tone)
- **Jacket lengths**: cropped (waist) lengthens legs; hip length is classic; longline (mid-thigh+) creates vertical line

## Necklines

| Neckline | Effect | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **V-neck / deep scoop** | Lengthens neck and torso, vertical line | Short necks, fuller bust, round faces |
| **Boat / bateau** | Widens shoulders | Narrow shoulders, triangle shapes |
| **Crew** | Neutral, youthful | Long necks, balanced shoulders |
| **Turtleneck** | Covers and elongates column | Long necks, monochrome looks |
| **Square** | Frames collarbones, adds structure | Softer or rounder faces; broad shoulders may prefer a V |
| **Halter** | Emphasizes shoulders and arms | Broad or athletic shoulders |
| **Off-shoulder** | Horizontal line, widens shoulders | Narrow shoulders |

## Scale Rules

- **Small frame** — prints under ~3 cm motifs, fine jewelry, slim belts, small bags
- **Medium frame** — medium-scale prints and accessories
- **Large frame / tall** — bold prints, chunky jewelry, oversized bags
- Break the rule deliberately for editorial impact; one oversized statement item, the rest in scale

## Outfit Formulas

Reusable outfit templates for lookbooks and capsules:

1. **Monochrome column** — same color top and bottom + contrasting long layer = maximum length
2. **Tuck + high rise + pointed shoe** — the rule-of-thirds leg lengthener
3. **Volume balance** — oversized knit + slim trouser + sleek shoe
4. **Third piece** — any basic outfit + a third layer (blazer, cardigan, vest, scarf) = finished look
5. **Neutral + one accent** — full neutral outfit + one piece in a palette accent color
6. **Texture mix** — tonal outfit with 3 textures (e.g., knit, satin, leather) adds depth without color

## Menswear-Specific Notes

- **Jacket shoulder** is the fit priority — hardest to alter
- **Trouser break** — no break (modern, cropped), quarter/half break (classic), full break (traditional)
- **Tie width** should relate to lapel width; lapel width to shoulder width
- **Shirt collar** — spread collars for round/wide faces balance, point collars for longer faces
- Vertical proportions matter for men too: high-rise trousers and shorter jackets lengthen the leg

## Adaptive and Inclusive Styling

- Seated wearers (wheelchair users): shorter front hems, longer back rise, no bulky back pockets, magnetic closures
- Modest dress: layering, long silhouettes, and color/texture play to express trend within coverage requirements
- Maternity: empire lines, stretch panels, wrap styles; volume balance still applies
- Always ask what the wearer values; comfort and function are part of good styling
