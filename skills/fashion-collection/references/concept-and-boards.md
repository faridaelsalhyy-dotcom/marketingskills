# Concept & Board Templates

Templates for the concept statement and the design boards that define a collection.

## Concept Worksheet

Answer before sketching:

1. **Inspiration source** — What exactly? (One primary source; up to two secondary.)
2. **Why now?** — Which macro trend or cultural moment makes it relevant?
3. **Point of view** — What is the designer's personal angle? What will this collection say?
4. **Customer** — Who wears it, where, and how do they feel in it?
5. **Design language** — Which 3 shapes, 3 materials, and 3 techniques express it?
6. **Brand DNA** — Which signature codes carry through?
7. **Keywords** — 3–5 words to test every design against (e.g., "protective, fluid, sun-bleached, utilitarian")
8. **What it is not** — 2–3 anti-keywords to avoid drift (e.g., "not costume, not beachwear")

### Concept Statement Examples

> **"Salt Road"** explores the textiles and workwear of Mediterranean salt harvesters through a lens of slow, sun-faded craft, translated into washed linens, crumpled cotton poplin, and tool-belt utility details for women who want city clothes that feel like summer holidays.

> **"Night Shift"** reinterprets hospital and transit uniforms as protective, soft tailoring — padded, quilted, and reflective — for urban commuters who live between day and night.

## Mood Board

- **Format** — landscape, A3 or 16:9 digital
- **Content** — 10–20 images: atmosphere, texture, color, light, architecture, people, objects — not other designers' garments
- **Text** — collection name, concept statement (1–2 lines), 3–5 keywords
- **Composition** — one dominant hero image; group images by color or mood; leave white space
- **Test** — someone unfamiliar with the concept should be able to describe its mood in 3 words that match your keywords

Balance the board across: **weight** (light / mid / heavy), **drape** (fluid / structured), **surface** (flat / textured / sheen), and include one hero or novelty fabric.

## Silhouette Board

- 3–5 silhouettes drawn as simple figure outlines (shape only)
- Label each (e.g., "Column," "Cocoon," "Cropped + wide")
- Note proportion intent (e.g., 1/3 : 2/3 cropped jacket over wide trouser)

## Details & Trims Board

- 4–8 signature details: close-up sketches or photos of samples
- For each detail, list which styles it appears on (aim for 3+)

## Line-Up Sheet

- All looks side by side, same scale, same croquis
- Numbered in running order
- Color-filled so palette flow is visible
- Mark hero looks
- Beneath each look: styles included, fabrics, colorway
